# AI Pattern Catalog (Universal)

Canonical, surface-agnostic catalog of AI-generated writing tells. Applies to any external-facing draft — LinkedIn posts, newsletters, email, investor updates, sales emails/texts.

Apply these rules to detect common AI patterns. If violations are found, edit or rewrite to fix them. Ensure changes still make sense given surrounding context. If there's no way to rewrite that makes sense, leave the violation but flag it.

Do not be overly literal about matches. If something is close, particularly any contrast framing, rewrite it.

---

## Forbidden Contrast Formats
- ❌ "This isn't about X—it's about Y"
- ❌ "This isn't about X; it's about Y"
- ❌ "It's not X, it's Y" / "It's not about X, it's about Y"
- ❌ "It wasn't X, it was Y"
- ❌ "These X haven't made Y more Z—they've made it W"
- ❌ "The problem isn't X—it's Y"
- ❌ "The issue isn't X, it's Y"

## Forbidden Robotic Transitions
- ❌ "Here's the thing:"
- ❌ "The reality is this:"
- ❌ "At the end of the day,"
- ❌ "The bottom line is:"
- ❌ "When all is said and done,"
- ❌ "The truth is,"
- ❌ "Let me be clear:"

## Forbidden AI Crutch Phrases
- ❌ "Let's dive deep into..."
- ❌ "Let's unpack this..."
- ❌ "Here's what you need to know:"
- ❌ "The key takeaway is..."
- ❌ "In today's digital landscape..."
- ❌ "In an ever-evolving world..."
- ❌ "In this day and age..."
- ❌ "Moving forward,"
- ❌ "here's the frame:" / "the frame that matters" / "here's the reframe:" / "the real reframe" — the frame/reframe-that-matters setup (an Opus tell). Ban "frame" and "reframe" as framing meta-commentary.

## Forbidden Generic List Introductions
- ❌ "Here are X ways to..."
- ❌ "Let me share X strategies..."
- ❌ "I'm going to break down X methods..."
- ❌ "Below are X tips to..."

## Forbidden Marketing Buzzwords & Clichés
- ❌ "discover," "unlock," "game-changer," "boost," "reveal," "supercharge"
- ❌ "you're not alone"
- ❌ "imagine" (as a directive to the reader)
- ❌ "changed everything," "rocked my world," "blew my mind" — overused transformation clichés that do no curiosity work
- ❌ "built differently" — high-frequency AI-generated phrase, banned regardless of context
- ❌ "quiet" as an insight descriptor — "a quiet problem," "the quiet issue," "quietly [verb]s" — dresses a plain claim up as a subtle discovery
- ❌ "load-bearing" — banned outright (e.g., "the load-bearing assumption," "load-bearing detail/word")
- ❌ "doing real work" / "does real work" — banned regardless of context; empty intensifier that asserts substance instead of showing it (e.g., "this sentence is doing real work," "the framing does real work")
- ❌ "shows up" with an abstract subject — "the failure shows up when...", "the cost shows up later", "that's where the risk shows up." AI's default move for making an abstraction feel concrete; instead say when/how the thing actually happens ("it's only wrong when...", "you pay for it in year three"). Physical/concrete arrivals are fine ("quarterly reports show up," "the invoice shows up in your inbox").
- ❌ "honest" as a self-characterizing adjective/adverb on the writer's own statement or analysis — "one honest caveat," "the honest answer," "to be honest," "honestly," "let me be honest," "in all honesty," and equally "an honest read," "the first honest mark," "an honest look at," "reading it honestly" — an Opus tell that labels its own candor or rigor instead of demonstrating it; cut the label and state the point directly, or say what specifically makes the read accurate. (Not a ban on "honest" describing people/things outside the writer's own claims, e.g. "honest sponsors.")

## Commonly Overused AI Words

Watch for unnaturally high density of these words. A single occurrence is fine; clusters are a red flag:

delve, delved, delving, navigate, navigated, navigating, leverage, leveraging, tapestry, beacon, pristine, intricate, profound, meticulous, testament, pivotal, nuanced, holistic, paradigm, synergy, landscape, ecosystem, foster, cultivate, empower, resonate, streamline, optimize, robust, comprehensive, innovative, transformative, curate, bespoke, seamless, endeavor, facilitate, encompass, underscore, forge, elevate

## Overused Adverbs

Flag if multiple appear in the same piece:

absolutely, carefully, cautiously, deliberately, essentially, fundamentally, incredibly, inevitably, ironically, meticulously, precisely, profoundly, remarkably, significantly, strategically, subsequently, ultimately

## Dash-Set-Off Framing Overuse
Setting off asides, appositives, and definitions with dashes as the default clause-joiner — an AI tell regardless of glyph (em dash, spaced en dash, hyphen). Swapping the em dash for an en dash does not fix it; the framing is the pattern.

**Check:** Count dash-set-off constructions. More than a couple per piece, or any stretch where the dash is the go-to move for attaching a clause, gets rewritten: asides into parentheses, appositives into commas, definitions and payoffs after colons, or split into separate sentences.

## Parallel Fragment Overuse
Consecutive short phrases or sentences with identical grammatical structure used as a rhythm device:
- ❌ "Track record. Decision-making. Alignment. Communication."
- ❌ "The market. The property. The business plan. The exit strategy."

**Rule:** Maximum ONE instance of parallel fragment lists (3+ consecutive items with identical structure) per piece. If the pattern appears more than once, rewrite subsequent instances into flowing prose. A single use can be effective for emphasis; repeated use is a strong AI fingerprint.

**Check:** Search the draft for clusters of 3+ short phrases ending in periods or using identical grammatical patterns. Count instances. If more than one, flag and rewrite.

## Forbidden Aphoristic Patterns (AI Sound Bites)
These pithy two-sentence constructions are dead giveaways. Ban them in hooks, openings, closings, and body text:
- ❌ "X don't do Y. They do Z." (e.g., "Sophisticated investors don't evaluate deals. They evaluate assumptions.")
- ❌ "The best X aren't Y. They just Z." (e.g., "The best investors aren't smarter. They just ask better questions.")
- ❌ "It's not about X. It's about Y."
- ❌ Any negate-then-reframe two-sentence pattern
- ❌ Chiastic structures that sound clever but read as AI-generated (e.g., "Don't invest in deals. Invest in operators.")
- ❌ Parallel negative/positive constructions used as hooks or closings

## Structural & Analytical Tells

### Superficial -ing phrase analysis
AI tacks on vague analytical commentary with present participles.

**Pattern:** "[fact], [present-participle phrase] the importance/need/value of [abstract noun]"

- ❌ "The study found a 20% increase, highlighting the importance of early intervention"
- ✅ "The study found a 20% increase in recovery rates when treatment began within 48 hours"

**Fix:** Replace the -ing analysis phrase with the specific implication stated as a fact.

### Editorializing phrases
AI adds meta-commentary about the content instead of just stating the content.

- ❌ "it's important to note / remember / consider"
- ❌ "worth [verb]-ing" as meta-commentary — "worth noting," "worth remembering," "worth pulling apart," "worth making," "worth considering." The "worth ___ing" frame tells the reader a thing matters instead of just showing it. Flag the whole construction, not only "worth noting."
- ❌ "no discussion would be complete without"
- ❌ "notably" (as filler)
- ❌ "interestingly" (as a sentence opener)

**Fix:** State the fact directly without telling the reader it's worth noting.

### Setup and recap sentences
AI announces what it's about to say, or restates what the reader already knows, instead of just saying the thing. Distinct from the editorializing phrases above: these are whole sentences or clauses that exist only to introduce or re-anchor the next sentence. A strong Opus tell, and the most common one in drafted email copy.

- ❌ "and there's a reason it's not just [X]" — announces that a justification follows
- ❌ "Here's the part nobody has checked yet." / "Here's the thing." / "Here's what I'd suggest." — pure runway before the actual point
- ❌ "You've checked the number three times." — recaps back to the recipient something they already know, as a setup for the instruction
- ❌ "One thing worth trying before we do anything else." — announces the suggestion instead of making it
- ❌ "Two notes, one of which matters." — pre-counts and pre-ranks what follows

**Fix:** Delete the sentence and start with the content it was introducing. If the setup carried real information (a caveat, a reason), fold that into the sentence it was propping up. Test: cut the sentence and read on — if nothing is lost, it was runway.

- ❌ "In summary, ..." / "In conclusion, ..." / "Overall, ..." / "To summarize, ..." / "In short, ..."
- ❌ "Ultimately, ..." (when used as a restatement, not as a meaningful word)

**Fix:** End with the most important or forward-looking information from that section, not a restatement.

### Significance self-assertion
Stamping importance on your own point instead of demonstrating it — the subtler cousin of "it's important to note," usually appended right after the writer's own claim or distinction.

- ❌ "and the distinction matters" / "the distinction matters here" / "that distinction matters"
- ❌ "and that matters" / "which matters more than it sounds"
- ❌ "this is an important distinction" / "the difference is important"

**Fix:** Cut the stamp and show the consequence directly. If the next sentence already demonstrates why the point matters, the stamp was runway; if it doesn't, write that sentence instead of the stamp.

### Document self-narration
The text announcing its own structure, purpose, or contents instead of delivering them. The document-level version of setup sentences — a strong Opus/Fable tell, and it survives outlines into drafts, so catch it in both.

- ❌ "What follows is the script." / "the script above" / "the list below"
- ❌ "This piece offers a way to..." / "So the piece offers..." / "In this newsletter we'll..."
- ❌ "As we'll see," / "more on that below" / "we'll get to that"

**Fix:** Delete the announcement; let headers and the argument's own sequence do the structural work. If the announcement carried a promise, restate it as the question or tension itself, not as a description of the document.

### Overused conjunctions
AI strings clauses together with the same connectives repeatedly.

- Watch for excessive: "moreover," "furthermore," "in addition," "additionally," "consequently," "on the other hand"

**Fix:** Combine into one flowing sentence, use varied natural transitions, or just start a new sentence with no connector.

### Generalizable puffery
- ❌ "stands as / serves as / is a testament"
- ❌ "plays a vital/significant role"
- ❌ "serves to [verb]"
- ❌ "seamlessly" (when describing how something works)

**Fix:** Replace with a specific, measurable fact about the subject.

### Vague attributions
AI cites unspecified experts or reports instead of real sources.

- ❌ "industry experts believe," "industry reports suggest," "observers note," "critics argue," "many say," "research suggests" (without citing actual research)

**Fix:** Provide the specific source, or state the claim directly without the fake attribution. (For first-person/deal claims, the domain-credibility Source Fidelity check is the stronger gate.)

## Framing Tells


### Invented concept labels
Appending an abstract noun to a domain term to manufacture analytical rigor. A heavy Opus tell.

**Pattern:** [domain noun] + paradox / trap / creep / divide / tax / economy / tell / problem
- ❌ "the supervision paradox," "the acceleration trap," "workload creep," "the alignment tax," "the trust divide"
- ✅ Describe the actual dynamic in plain words.

**Fix:** State what's actually happening. If the coined label isn't a real, established term of art, cut it.

### False ranges
"From X to Y" implies a spectrum but lists unrelated concepts with no meaningful middle.
- ❌ "everything from innovation to cultural transformation," "from tax strategy to legacy building"
- ✅ Name the specific items, or drop the false-spectrum framing entirely.

### Grandiose stakes inflation
Framing a mundane topic as a civilization-level inflection point. Qualitative check — flag when the stakes claimed wildly exceed the subject (a routine deal update framed as "the future of American energy," a pricing note framed as "a historic shift").

**Fix:** Match the framing to the actual stakes. Let a small point be a small point.

---

## Natural Alternatives
- Start directly with the actual point
- Write conversationally like talking to a friend
- Use specific examples and concrete scenarios
- Vary sentence structure and length naturally
- Let personality shine through with authentic voice
- Jump straight into value without robotic setups

## Final AI Detection Test
Read the draft aloud. Ask: "Would a human naturally say this exact phrase in conversation?" If it sounds like every other AI-generated article, rewrite it completely.
